# Research basis and evaluation

The design treats intentional friction as an intervention that makes a meaningful human judgment happen and affect subsequent work. It does not treat extra clicks, compulsory essays, or delays as evidence of deliberation.

Useful primary sources:

- [To Trust or to Think (2021)](https://www.eecs.harvard.edu/~kgajos/papers/2021/bucinca2021trust.shtml): cognitive forcing reduced overreliance in the studied task, with usability trade-offs and participant differences.
- [Better AI with Designed Friction (2025)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/full/10.3233/FAIA250680): a research agenda for deliberate interventions that support critical engagement and agency.
- [Drag or Traction (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.27550): a six-designer qualitative study of friction in ideation; resistance was not productive for everyone.
- [AI and actor-specific decisions (2026)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00146-026-03202-7): a preliminary normative account of decisions involving an actor's goals, theories, and commitments.

The mapping from these works to this library is a design proposal. None evaluates friction-kit or demonstrates long-term skill preservation in its users.

Evaluate task outcome, alignment with the person's criteria, later independent performance, and burden separately. Compare equivalent tasks with and without the intervention. Include correct and incorrect AI suggestions, and check whether people accept useful advice as well as reject harmful advice. Track whether the recorded choice actually changed dependent work. Measure abandonment, unnecessary interruption, and time alongside error detection.

Start with a concrete decision point: choosing a problem, comparing design directions, diagnosing an unexpected result, selecting the next experiment, or adopting a consequential action. Avoid a global score declaring a user to have thought enough.
